# M6 — Audio visualizer: brief

- **Status:** approved by owner 2026-11-01 ("could we do the audio visualizer milestone first?").
- **Goal:** a small, calm visualizer for the playing track, driven by the actual audio. It must stay within the mpv-over-IPC architecture: no second decoder for playback, and no system audio capture.
- **Delivery:** slice 1 is a timeboxed feasibility spike with written findings. Slice 2 is the feature, briefed in detail from those findings.

## Slice 1 — feasibility spike

### Questions
1. **Levels.** Can mpv report per-channel RMS and peak levels while playing?
   - Candidate: `af=@viz:lavfi=[astats=metadata=1:reset=…]`, then read or observe `af-metadata/viz` over IPC.
   - Measure: the update rate, IPC traffic, and the CPU cost in mpv and in Coda.
2. **Frequency bands.** Can mpv report the levels of about 8–16 frequency bands, with the played audio left bit-identical?
   - Candidate graph: `asplit` → one `bandpass` (or `lowpass`/`highpass`) branch per band → `amerge` the bands as extra channels next to the original → one `astats` with per-channel metadata → `pan` back to the original channel layout.
   - Verify:
     - that the frame metadata survives `pan`;
     - that the output is the untouched original audio;
     - that the latency is not increased;
     - how this works for mono, stereo and 5.1 input.
   - Try other ideas if this fails: `aspectralstats`, `ebur128`, `lavfi-complex`.
3. **Timing.** How far ahead of the speaker is the analysed audio (the audio-output buffer, cf. LESSONS "mpv's `end-file eof` is not the audio finished")? Can Coda compensate, e.g. by delaying values by `audio-out-delay`/`ao-delay`-like properties or by the measured AO buffer? Is the lag visible without compensation?
4. **Lifecycle.**
   - Is the filter added once at start and kept across `loadfile replace`?
   - Can it be toggled at runtime (`af add`/`af remove`) without a click or gap?
   - What happens with streams, with seeks, with pauses, and with odd sample rates, channel counts and codecs?
   - What happens when the ffmpeg build lacks a filter? It must degrade to "no visualizer", never break playback.
5. **Cost.** CPU usage of mpv with and without the filter on this Mac (and in the Linux container if possible), and the Coda-side cost at 30 updates per second.

### Deliverable
- **Findings section.** A "Findings" section appended to this brief, giving for each question the answer, the evidence (commands, numbers, mpv version) and the open risks. End it with a recommendation for slice 2: what the visualizer can show (meter, bands, or both), at what rate, and how it attaches to the backend.
- **Throwaway code.** Prototype code may live in the scratchpad, or behind an `#[ignore]` real-mpv test. Do not change the shipping playback path in the spike, apart from test-only hooks if needed.
- **Tests.** Real-mpv probes use generated test signals (sine sweeps or tones at known frequencies), so the band levels can be checked against expectations.

## Slice 2 — visualizer (briefed after slice 1)
These constraints already hold:
- **Where it shows:** in the now-playing area, toggled with `v` (provisional, settled in M7). Off by default until the owner decides otherwise; the toggle is saved in the session.
- **No work when nothing is shown.** No updates and no redraws while it is hidden, paused or stopped, or while no track is playing. While visible, cap updates at about 30 per second and draw only the visualizer area.
- **Untrusted input.** Metadata values are parsed defensively: bounded numbers, NaN and infinity rejected, missing keys tolerated.
- **Behind the backend boundary.** All of it stays behind the playback boundary (`PlaybackEvent`/`PlaybackCommand`), with no mpv details in the UI.
- **Rendering.** Unicode block characters, with sensible behaviour at small widths and in `NO_COLOR`/16-colour terminals.

## Findings (slice 1 spike, 2026-11-01)

### Setup
- **macOS:** mpv 0.41.0 with FFmpeg 9.0.2 (Homebrew), on an Apple M1 Pro.
- **Linux:** mpv 0.35.1 with FFmpeg 5.1.9, in a `debian:bookworm-slim` arm64 container (the same Debian release as `rust:1-bookworm`, without the toolchain). The container has no sound device, so it used only `--ao=null` and `--ao=pcm`.
- **mpv instances:** every probe spawns its own mpv with Coda's base arguments (`--no-config --idle=yes --no-video --no-terminal --ytdl=no --network-timeout=15 --access-references=no`) and drives it over JSON IPC from Python.
- **Device runs:** real-device runs use `--ao=coreaudio --volume=0`, so they are silent. mpv's volume is applied after the user filters, so the filter still sees full-scale audio.
- **Test audio:** generated by ffmpeg only:
  - a tone sequence: 2 s each of 60, 250, 1000, 4000 and 12000 Hz;
  - pink noise in mono, stereo and 5.1;
  - a 60 s FLAC;
  - odd formats: 8 kHz `u8` mono WAV, 96 kHz 24-bit FLAC, 22 kHz MP3, Opus, 7.1 `f32` WAV and AAC in `.m4a`.
- **Coda's code:** no Coda source or test files were changed.
- **Bit-identity method:** the audio is rendered twice with `--ao=pcm --ao-pcm-waveheader=no`, with and without the filter, and the two outputs are compared byte for byte.

### Q1: Levels
- **Answer:** yes. `--af=@viz:lavfi=[astats=metadata=1:reset=1:measure_perchannel=RMS_level+Peak_level:measure_overall=none]` exposes `af-metadata/viz` as a map of strings, e.g. `{"lavfi.astats.1.RMS_level":"-23.08", "lavfi.astats.1.Peak_level":"-12.97", …}`.
  - There is one key per channel and statistic, so 6 channels for 5.1 and 8 for 7.1.
  - Digital silence gives `"-inf"`.
  - The output is bit-identical for mono, stereo and 5.1 WAV and for FLAC. `u8` is the exception (see Q4). The odd formats were checked byte for byte only with the band graph, and pass.
- **Update rate:** each update is one filtered frame, so the rate depends on the decoder's frame size unless the frames are re-chunked.
  - Without re-chunking: WAV gives about 10–11 updates/s and FLAC or Opus about 17/s.
  - With `asetnsamples=n=1470:p=0` first, CoreAudio gives 30 updates/s and `n=441` gives 80/s.
  - `--ao=null` caps at about 19/s, because it pulls audio in bursts.
- **Polling versus `observe_property`:** use polling.
  - `observe_property af-metadata/viz` delivered at most about 15–19 changes/s, with a minimum gap of about 50 ms, even when the value changed 30–80 times a second.
  - `get_property` polling at 30 Hz saw every value (54 of 54 distinct). The median round trip was 0.5 ms and the worst about 7 ms.
  - Observation also keeps sending while the visualizer is hidden, unless the client unobserves.
- **Cost:** about 0.7–1.5 % of one core on top of playback.
  - macOS, CoreAudio, 60 s FLAC: mpv used 7.8 % without the filter and 8.7–9.1 % with levels polled at 30 Hz.
  - Linux, `ao=null`: 3.2 % without and 4.7 % with.
  - IPC traffic at 30 Hz is about 5.5 KB/s.
- **Risks:**
  - A value lingers after pause (see Q4).
  - Values are strings that can be `-inf`, so they must be parsed defensively.

### Q2: Frequency bands
- **The brief's graph works,** but only when the `pan` matches the decoded channel layout. The tested graph (`scratchpad/m6spike/graphs.py`, `amerge_pan`) is:
  `asetnsamples=n=1470:p=0,asplit[o][a];[a]aformat=channel_layouts=mono,asplit=9[b0]…[b8];[bN]bandpass=f=F:width_type=o:w=1[fN]…;[f0]…[f8]amerge=inputs=9[bands];[o][bands]amerge=inputs=2,astats=metadata=1:reset=1:measure_perchannel=RMS_level+Peak_level:measure_overall=none,pan=stereo|c0=c0|c1=c1`.
  - **Bit-identical** on mpv 0.41: mono with `pan=mono`, stereo, 5.1 with the 5.1 `pan`, 44.1 kHz FLAC (decoded as `s32`), 96 kHz `s32`, MP3, Opus and AAC. Removing `asetnsamples` gives the same result.
  - **Metadata survives `pan`.** `amerge` drops metadata, so `astats` must come after the merge, as in the brief.
  - **No added latency:** the output starts at the same sample and has the same length. Time from `loadfile` to `playback-restart` was 7.5 ms without the graph and 9.1 ms with it (medians over 8 loads).
- **Band accuracy:** the band levels match the known tones.
  - With 9 octave bands (63 Hz to 16 kHz, 2-pole `bandpass`, `w=1` octave), each tone peaks in its own band at the input level of −21 dB.
  - Neighbouring bands fall about 7 dB per octave. That leakage is fine for a calm display.
  - A 12 kHz tone sits between the 8 kHz band (−25.7 dB) and the 16 kHz band (−22.2 dB).
  - Linux, with FFmpeg 5.1, gives the same numbers.
- **The blocker:** `pan` must name the layout, and lavfi has no layout-agnostic way to drop the extra channels. A fixed graph applied to a different layout silently damages the audio:
  - stereo `pan` on mono: the 63 Hz band plays as the right channel;
  - stereo `pan` on 5.1: the surround channels are dropped;
  - mono `pan` on stereo: the right channel is dropped;
  - a `pan` that names missing channels: silence, with mpv still reporting success.
- **Older stacks:** on mpv 0.35 with FFmpeg 5.1, the `amerge` graph on a mono file dropped the first 2048 samples (43 ms), even with the matching `pan`. The rest was identical.
- **Alternatives tried:**
  - **Two-input filters** (`asdr`, `apsnr`, `amix`, `sidechaingate`, `ainterleave`): none passes one input's samples with the other's metadata.
  - **Per-layout graph chosen in an `on_preloaded` hook:** `hook_add` over JSON IPC returns `invalid parameter` in mpv 0.41. Rejected. An in-process Lua script could do it, but that adds a component.
  - **Analysis branch into a sink** (`…astats,ametadata=mode=print:file=…,anullsink;[o]anull`): generic and bit-identical for every layout. However, the values go to a file, outside IPC, and the file grows by about 30 KB/s. A FIFO would stall mpv's audio thread whenever Coda stops reading. Not recommended.
  - **`aspectralstats`:** generic, but it gives spectral shape (centroid, flux and so on), not bands. A fallback at most.
  - **Normalise to stereo with mpv's own `format` filter** (the recommended route): `--audio-channels=stereo` combined with `--af=format=channels=stereo,@viz:lavfi=[<stereo graph>]`.
    - One fixed graph then fits every track.
    - The output is byte-identical to plain `--audio-channels=stereo` for mono, 5.1 and 7.1 on both mpv 0.41 and 0.35. On 0.35 this also avoids the 2048-sample mono loss, because the graph never sees mono.
- **Risks of the stereo route:**
  - Coda would always output stereo, with mpv's downmix. This is a product decision; see the end of this section.
  - Mono becomes L = R at −3 dB each, which is mpv's own upmix (checked by sample: 1244/1760 ≈ 0.707).
  - On this Mac, mpv already downmixes 5.1 to stereo for the device. Mono, by contrast, currently goes to `avfoundation`, the slow output from LESSONS. With `--audio-channels=stereo`, mono goes to CoreAudio instead (observed through `current-ao`). That plausibly removes the 4 s avfoundation buffer, but it is not verified on Bluetooth.
  - Bands above Nyquist return the full-band level, e.g. the 16 kHz band on 8 kHz audio. Coda must hide bands at or above `samplerate/2`, which it can read from `audio-params/samplerate`.

### Q3: Timing
- **The analysed audio runs ahead of what is heard.** The analysed audio leads mpv's own `time-pos` (which already accounts for the output's reported delay) by:

  | Output | `--audio-buffer` | Lead |
  |---|---|---|
  | CoreAudio | default 0.2 s | about 235 ms (tone edges) / 263 ms (time code) |
  | CoreAudio | 0.05 s | about 100–130 ms |
  | `ao=null` | default | about 370–410 ms |
  | `avfoundation` | default | 2.5–3.0 s |

  - "Tone edges" means the moment the loudest band switches, compared with the moment `time-pos` crosses the 2 s boundary.
  - Without asetnsamples the lead also jitters by about ±50 ms (278–383 ms).
  - 235 ms is clearly visible: bars move a quarter-second before the beat. Compensation is needed.
- **No mpv property exposes the output latency,** neither in `--list-properties` nor otherwise.
- **Compensation that works:** a time-code channel. Add `[t]aeval=exprs=mod(t\,32)/32[tc]` as one more `amerge` input and read its `Peak_level`:
  - `t = 32·10^(dB/20)`, unwrapped against `time-pos`.
  - `Peak_level` is dBFS for every sample format. The raw `Max_level` is not: its scale differs between `s16`, `s32` and float.
  - The output stays bit-identical.
  - Measured lead: 262–264 ms on CoreAudio for WAV `s16`, FLAC `s32` and AAC `floatp`, and 408 ms on Linux `ao=null`.
  - The time code is stable across `loadfile`, a seek to 45 s and one to 3 s. Within 0.3 s of each restart it reads 204–209 ms, then settles at 262 ms.
  - The time code is also a free way to measure the lead in slice-2 tests.
- **How Coda can use it:** keep a short queue of `(pts, values)` and show the newest entry whose pts is at or before the extrapolated `time-pos`.
- **A simpler fallback:** a fixed delay of about 0.2–0.25 s. It is right for CoreAudio's default buffer and wrong by seconds on `avfoundation`.
- **Risks:**
  - The time code needs the `amerge` graph, so it is not available with levels alone.
  - Linux sound servers (PulseAudio, PipeWire) were not measured, because the container has no device.
  - `time-pos` itself trusts the delay the output reports. Nothing was measured acoustically.

### Q4: Lifecycle
- **`loadfile replace`:** a filter set at spawn stays across loads, and metadata keeps flowing for mono, stereo, FLAC and 5.1 in a row.
- **Runtime toggling:** measured with a non-realtime `ao=pcm` render of a 60 s `s32` FLAC.
  - `af add` during playback is lossless: the output is byte-identical to the unfiltered one.
  - `af remove` drops the audio buffered inside the graph: 1024 frames (23 ms) for levels and 2424 frames (55 ms) for the band graph. That is an audible skip.
  - `af set` to the same graph is a no-op.
  - In real time on CoreAudio: replies took 1–5 ms, there were no underruns in a `-v` log, and `time-pos` stayed within ±20 ms of the wall clock.
- **Validation depends on when the filter is added.**
  - While audio plays, `af add` builds the graph. A graph naming a missing filter (`nosuchfilter_xyz`) is rejected with `error running command`, `af` stays empty, and playback continues.
  - At spawn, or with `af add` while idle, a bad graph is accepted unchecked, and then *every* file fails with `end-file reason=error, file_error="no audio or video data played"`. This was reproduced on mpv 0.41 and 0.35. `af remove` recovers.
  - An unknown mpv-level filter name in `--af` makes mpv exit at startup.
  - An out-of-process preflight, `mpv --no-config --no-terminal --no-video --ao=null --af=<graph> av://lavfi:anullsrc=d=0.1:cl=stereo`, exits 0 for a good graph and 2 for a bad one, in about 0.3 s. It works on both versions. It needs mpv built with libavdevice. Otherwise it fails closed: no visualizer.
- **Pause, stop and seek:**
  - While paused there are no updates, but the last value stays readable, so Coda must ignore it.
  - After `stop` or when idle, the property is unavailable (`None`).
  - After a seek, `playback-restart` comes 3–7 ms after the command, and new metadata follows 12–32 ms later.
- **Streams:** HTTP from a local `http.server` works. FLAC, MP3 and WAV over HTTP give the same update rates as local files, including after a 2 s seek.
- **Odd formats:**
  - 8 kHz `u8` mono: about 6 updates/s, with a 1470-sample chunk.
  - 96 kHz `s32`, 22 kHz MP3, Opus, 7.1 `f32` and AAC: 16–19 updates/s under `ao=null`, with no non-finite values.
  - Format change for `u8`: `astats` has no `u8` support, so lavfi converts to `s16` and mpv outputs `s16`. The values are identical (`(x−128)<<8`, checked), but the bytes are not.
- **Risks:**
  - The "bad graph breaks all playback" failure mode is the main hazard. Never attach the graph unvalidated at spawn or while idle.
  - Removing the graph mid-track skips 23–55 ms.

### Q5: Cost
Costs are the mpv process's CPU as a percentage of one core, over 12–15 s of a 60 s 44.1 kHz FLAC. The noise between runs is about ±1 %.

| Variant | macOS, CoreAudio | Linux, `ao=null` |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline, no filter | 5.5–7.9 % | 3.1–3.2 % |
| Levels | +0.7–1.3 % | +1.5 % |
| 9 bands, chunk 1470 | +3.5–6 % | +6 % |
| Candidate graph (7 bands, L/R, time code), chunk 1024 | +5.5–6.5 % | +6.6 % |
| Candidate graph, chunk 2048 | +3.4 % | +5.6 % |
| Candidate graph, chunk 4096 | +1 % | not measured |

- **What the cost depends on:** the number of frames per second, not the band filters. Replacing the 7 `bandpass` filters with `anull` cost the same. Removing `astats` brought it close to the baseline.
- **Polling at 30 Hz:** adds nothing measurable to mpv. Traffic is about 23–25 KB/s for the band graph.
- **Coda-side cost:** a Rust `serde_json` parse of a real 939-byte reply, plus defensive float extraction of 11 values, takes 3.3 µs. At 30 Hz that is 0.01 % of a core (`scratchpad/m6spike/parsebench`).

### Recommendation for slice 2
- **What to show:**
  - 7 bands (63, 160, 400, 1000, 2500, 6300 and 16000 Hz; `bandpass`, `w=1.3` octaves) from a mono downmix;
  - plus an L/R level meter.
- **The graph:** one fixed graph, verified bit-identical on both mpv versions (`graphs.py`, `recommended`):
  `format=channels=stereo,@viz:lavfi=[asetnsamples=n=<N>:p=0,asplit[o][a];[a]aformat=channel_layouts=mono,asplit=8[b0]…[b6][t];[bI]bandpass=f=F:width_type=o:w=1.3[fI]…;[t]aeval=exprs=mod(t\,32)/32[tc];[o][f0]…[f6][tc]amerge=inputs=9,astats=metadata=1:reset=1:measure_perchannel=RMS_level+Peak_level:measure_overall=none,pan=stereo|c0=c0|c1=c1]`.
  - It needs `--audio-channels=stereo` at spawn, always, so that attaching the visualizer never changes what is heard.
- **Rate:**
  - `N=2048` gives 21–23 updates/s at 44.1/48 kHz and costs about 3.5–5.5 % of a core while attached.
  - `N=1024` gives about 43 updates/s and costs about 6 %.
  - Coda polls `get_property af-metadata/viz` at most at about 30 Hz, with one request in flight. It polls only while the visualizer is visible and playback is playing. It uses polling rather than observation.
- **Attaching:**
  - Send `af add` lazily, the first time the visualizer is visible while a track is *playing*. Adding is lossless, and mpv validates the graph then. An error reply means "visualizer unavailable" for the session.
  - Never pass the graph at spawn, and never add it while idle.
  - When the visualizer is hidden, stop polling at once. Detach with `af remove` only at the next track change or stop, to avoid the 23–55 ms skip.
- **The backend boundary:**
  - Add `PlaybackCommand::SetVisualizer(bool)`.
  - The mpv driver owns the poll timer, the parsing, the time-code alignment and the hiding of bands at or above Nyquist.
  - It emits a backend-neutral, coalesced `PlaybackEvent::Visualizer { load: LoadId, levels: [f32; 2], bands: [f32; 7] }`, normalised to 0..1 from clamped dBFS, with non-finite values mapped to silence.
  - It reports unavailability once.
  - The UI never sees mpv keys.

### Decisions for the architect and owner
1. **Is always-stereo output acceptable?** `--audio-channels=stereo` is what makes one safe bands graph possible. It loses surround on multichannel devices, and mono plays as −3 dB L = R. On this Mac it also moves mono from `avfoundation` to CoreAudio. If it is not acceptable, slice 2 can ship L/R levels only:
   - plain `astats`, generic for every layout;
   - a fixed 0.2–0.25 s delay instead of the time code;
   - about 1 % CPU.
2. **Hidden but attached:** is keeping the graph attached while hidden acceptable until the next track change? That costs about 3.5–6 % of a core and avoids a mid-track skip.
3. **Linux timing:** the Linux PulseAudio and PipeWire lead was not measured. Check it on a real Linux desktop during slice 2.

Scripts and generated-audio recipes are in the session scratchpad under `m6spike/`. They are throwaway and not committed.
